Project overview · November 2026

STAIR for Investment Research: A Structure-Aware Retriever as the Foundation for an Analyst Agent over SEC 10-K Filings

Topic

STAIR is a fine-tuned LLM that retrieves information by picking the right section from a document’s table of contents, instead of searching text chunks the way standard retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) does. This project applies STAIR to annual 10-K filings, which are long but follow a fixed, well-defined structure. Accurate, cited retrieval is the core that any investment-research agent over filings depends on.

Goal

Answer the question the STAIR paper leaves open in its Section 7: does STAIR work zero-shot, on documents it never trained on? The paper trains and tests on the same books, so its 82.6% Recall@1 relies on having memorised each book. Here STAIR trains on FY2024 filings only and is tested on FY2025 filings at two levels: temporal zero-shot (a new filing from a company seen in training) and cross-company zero-shot (a company it never saw). 10-Ks are the best case for this, because their structure is fixed by law. The project compares STAIR with standard RAG retrieval, and demonstrates it inside a simple analyst workflow: cited answers and a due-diligence checklist across companies.

Objectives

  1. 1
    Build a 10-K question dataset. Split each company’s 10-Ks into tables of contents and section text, and generate questions per section with an LLM, each labelled with its section: training questions from the FY2024 filings, test questions from FY2025. Hand-written test set: 7 FinanceBench analyst questions and 8 checklist questions asked of all 16 companies’ FY2025 filings, plus 56 company-specific questions weighted toward the 8 unseen companies (about 280 questions). No question names the company. Done when: all 24 filings have a clean table of contents, the splits are made, and 8 companies are held out: 2 first-time filers, 6 established.
  2. 2
    Train STAIR on the filings. Fine-tune Mistral-7B-Instruct with QLoRA, on the authors’ official code, to pick the right section from the table of contents. It trains on the FY2024 filings only, for at most 5 epochs, with no company names in its prompts, so it learns to read tables of contents rather than memorise filings. Done when: the checkpoint is chosen by dev Recall@1, and STAIR returns a ranked top 3 for any question.
  3. 3
    Compare STAIR with standard RAG: BM25, embedding retrieval, their hybrid, and the hybrid with a reranker (standard and modern RAG), plus STAIR’s own base model, untuned, given the table of contents. None of these is trained, and every method is tested on the FY2025 filings, which STAIR never trained on. Measured by: Recall@1 against hybrid + reranker, in two co-primary comparisons fixed in advance (temporal and cross-company zero-shot); Recall@3, invalid outputs, and cost and time per question.
  4. 4
    Test whether it holds up on new filings. Built into the main test: STAIR trained on FY2024 answers questions about FY2025 filings, for 8 companies it saw the year before and 8 it never saw (2 first-time filers, 6 established companies). Measured by: accuracy on new, renamed and unchanged sections, and on companies it never saw.
  5. 5
    Show the investment use. Run a fixed 12-question due-diligence checklist across all companies with STAIR’s top sections, and build a year-over-year signal table with every score citing its section. Measured by: a hand check of 20 answers, covering agreement with my own scores and whether each quote and citation is valid.

Roadmap: from retriever to analyst agent

This project measures single-shot STAIR: one pass over the table of contents. The paper’s Section 7 envisions STAIR iterating over leaf nodes and reasoning on their content; the gap between Recall@1 and Recall@3 hints at what that would add. After this project, STAIR becomes the retrieval tool of such an agent: it splits an analyst’s question into steps, reads STAIR’s top sections, checks whether they answer it, looks further when they don’t, and answers with citations. Later steps: 25–30 companies, 10-Qs and XBRL figures, more sectors, and automatic retraining each filing season.

Out of scope for this project: the agent itself, predicting stock prices, and testing trading returns. The project builds and measures the retrieval and signal step they all need.
